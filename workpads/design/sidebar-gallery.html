argus · sidebar design gallery

Eight ways to navigate projects → workflows → runs. The shipped tree wins on hierarchy, but argus exists to show what your agents are doing — so we weigh two more lenses.

Hierarchy

Which workflow does this run belong to? Containment must be obvious, not re-joined by a repeated name.

Time

What ran when? Running → recent → older. The "what happened" story across all workflows.

Activity

What are my agents doing right now? Running runs should be loud, not buried in a collapsed folder.

2 · Chronological 254544 Best "what happened" scan; loses the hierarchy you asked to make obvious.
3 · Grouped headers 533443 Hierarchy without twisties; sticky opaque headers are CSS-fragile.
4 · Two-pane (Miller) 433432 Focused, but defers runs behind a click & costs width in a 264px rail.
5 · Group-by toggle 554443 One sidebar, every lens on demand. Most flexible; adds a little state.
6 · Activity-first 345443 Nails the core objective (live monitoring); hierarchy demoted to the bottom.
7 · Two-col double-collapse 543322 Hierarchy + time together & dense; novel and width-hungry.
8 · Timeline feed 254433 Best chronological narrative; weak hierarchy, heavier to lay out.

Recommendation

No single fixed list wins all three lenses — and that's the real finding. Hierarchy and time are genuinely orthogonal questions ("which workflow?" vs "what happened when?"), so forcing one layout to answer both is why every single-shape design scores a 2 somewhere.

Ship #5 Group-by toggle as the frame, keeping the shipped Tree as its default "Workflow" grouping (zero regression), and adding Time and Status groupings that re-bucket the exact same run rows. Fold in #6 Activity-first's loud NOW region as a constant pinned-top band across all three groupings — that's the one element the core objective ("what are my agents doing") demands regardless of lens, and it generalizes the LIVE region the tree already has.

Keep #7 two-column double-collapse in reserve: its time-bucket idea (Today / This week / Older, collapsible) is exactly what the "Time" grouping needs — we get its best idea inside one column without paying its width or novelty cost. #2 / #8 are strong but redundant once "Time" grouping exists; #3 / #4 are dominated by the tree.
